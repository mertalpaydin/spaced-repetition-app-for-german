**The gate is not passed. Do not run the full top-up.**

Post-verifier error rate on the accepted set: **13 of 46, or 28.3%**, against a threshold of roughly 15% (`00-index.md:32`). ## The finding that matters

**Every defect falls in a topic the verification chain cannot check. Every topic the chain does check came out clean.**

| Topic | Accepted | Defects | Chain coverage |
|---|---|---|---|
| `adjektivdeklination_nullartikel` | 8 | 3 | none: `morph_spec` is `{}`, so layer 2 skips it entirely |
| `diskurs_adverbialanschluss` | 6 | 3 | none: connector semantics are not rule-checkable |
| `funktionsverbgefuege` | 5 | 2 | none: collocation correctness is not rule-checkable |
| `adjektiv_feste_praepositionen` | 3 | 2 | none |
| `konjunktiv_ii_irreal_gegenwart` | 5 | 2 | partial |
| `kasus_genitiv_formen` | 7 | 0 | yes |
| `futur_i` | 3 | 0 | yes |
| `artikel_*` (three topics) | 7 | 1 | yes |
| `adjektivdeklination_bestimmt`, `adjektiv_komparativ_superlativ` | 2 | 0 | yes |

Roughly 20 items in rule-checkable topics produced zero morphological defects. Roughly 22 items in unchecked topics produced 12. That is not a generator quality problem in the general sense; it is a coverage problem with a precise shape.

Two specific holes account for almost all of it:

1. **Layer 2 morphology still covers three topic ids.** `layer2_morphology.py` was generalised off `topic.id` onto `morph_spec`, but a topic with an empty `morph_spec` is skipped by design, and strong adjective declension without an article is exactly such a topic. It is also exactly where gender agreement errors live. Items 14 and 15 are textbook strong-declension errors that a paradigm check would have caught outright.

2. **The answer-set expansion layer is still dead.** `layer_expander.py` is a 16-entry hardcoded contraction table that `pipeline.py` never calls. The plan specifies this as the one model-backed layer in the chain. Semantic faults, wrong connectors, broken collocations and incoherent carriers are precisely what a cheap batched model pass catches and what no regex will. Items 34, 35, 39, 40, 41, 43 and 44 are all in that class.

## Also observed

- **31 of 54 rejections were `structural_malformation`**, the single largest bucket. The pilot does not persist rejected items, so the cause cannot be diagnosed from this run. That is a gap in the pilot harness: if the model is emitting malformed output a third of the time, prompt or parsing needs work; if the check is over-strict, good content is being discarded. Both are worth knowing and neither is currently knowable.
- **Cost was 0.00 USD** because the free lane prices at zero by design. The spend ceiling is therefore still untested against a real charge.
- Facet derivation returned `Unk` on several accepted items in covered topics, for example `wird` in `futur_i` yielding `Number=Unk|Person=Unk`. It does not affect this gate, but it will weaken promotion evidence later.

## Recommendation

Do not generate the remaining stock. In order:

1. **Persist rejected candidates** with their reason, so the 31 structural rejections can be diagnosed. Cheap, and it unblocks the next question.
2. **Wire `layer_expander` into the chain** as the plan specifies. This is the missing layer, and it is the one that addresses the largest defect class.
3. **Extend layer 2 to strong adjective declension**, driven by the determiner context that `facets.py` already parses for facet derivation. The paradigm tables exist; they are just not used for verification.
4. **Re-run the pilot** and re-audit. Add a second independent auditor for the re-run, per the agent-critical protocol.
5. Until the chain covers them, consider excluding the collocation and discourse topics from bulk generation, or routing them through a human check.

The three A1 and A2 morphology topics are already generating cleanly and could be topped up ahead of the rest if stock is needed sooner.
